🔬 Abstract  
Viral exanthems are common pediatric dermatological conditions that often accompany viral infections and are a frequent cause for medical consultations. The pathogenesis involves immune responses triggered by viruses like influenza, enteroviruses, parvovirus B19, varicella-zoster virus, and measles/rubella viruses. These exanthems present in various forms, including maculopapular, vesicular, and petechial rashes. Diagnosis is crucial for appropriate management, which generally includes symptomatic treatment. Vaccination remains the most effective preventive strategy against vaccine-preventable diseases like measles and rubella. The clinical approach to managing these conditions in outpatient settings includes symptom control, hydration, fever management, and sometimes antiviral therapy. This study highlights the importance of early detection, appropriate treatment, and vaccination in managing viral exanthems, helping to minimize unnecessary antibiotic use.
